The Solow Room

Robert M. Solow
1924 – 2023
Nobel Laureate in Economics, 1987
MIT Department of Economics
President, American Economic Association, 1979

Robert M. Solow showed that the long-run path of an economy need not exhaust itself against resource limits — if capital accumulation and technological progress move fast enough, each generation can bequeath the next at least as much productive capacity as it inherited. His 1974 paper Intergenerational Equity and Exhaustible Resources made this intuition rigorous, establishing the sustainability condition — later formalised as the Hartwick Rule — that still anchors resource economics today. The Solow Room collects theoretical work in this tradition: dynamic optimisation models, capital theory, shadow pricing, and the mathematics of scarcity over time. Papers here ask not what we found in the data, but what the structure of the problem itself implies — and why that structure matters for the choices every generation must make about resources it did not create and cannot replace.

SOLOW ROOM
Solow Room · 1974 Prices vs. Quantities

When a regulator faces uncertainty about the costs of abatement, should it set a price on pollution or impose a quantity limit? Weitzman's answer — that the choice depends on the relative slopes of the marginal benefit and cost curves — remains the foundational result in instrument choice under uncertainty, and still drives every carbon pricing debate.

Martin L. Weitzman Quarterly Journal of Economics 1974 Q5 D8
SOLOW ROOM
Solow Room · 1931 The Economics of Exhaustible Resources

The price of a non-renewable resource in a competitive market must rise at the rate of interest. This is the foundational result in resource economics — every subsequent extraction model either builds on Hotelling's rule or argues with it. Fifty years later it remains both indispensable and contested.

Harold Hotelling Journal of Political Economy 1931 Q3 Q5
SOLOW ROOM
Solow Room · 1974 Intergenerational Equity and Exhaustible Resources

If utility must be non-decreasing across generations and a resource is essential to production, what extraction path is optimal? Solow's answer defines the sustainability constraint that anchors the Hartwick rule and still frames debates about green national accounts and genuine savings today.

Robert M. Solow Review of Economic Studies 1974 Q0 D9
SOLOW ROOM
Solow Room · 1974 On the Optimal Depletion of Exhaustible Resources

A rigorous treatment of the optimal control problem for resource extraction, characterising the shadow price path and the conditions under which backstop technologies alter the extraction decision. The paper that showed Hotelling's rule survives in a full general equilibrium framework.

Partha Dasgupta & Geoffrey Heal Review of Economic Studies 1974 Q3 C6
SOLOW ROOM
Solow Room · 2001 Gamma Discounting

When there is genuine uncertainty about the appropriate discount rate, the effective social discount rate should decline over time — a result with large consequences for how we value long-run environmental investments and obligations to future generations. Weitzman's approach resolves a long-standing puzzle about declining discount rates without arbitrariness.

Martin L. Weitzman American Economic Review 2001 Q5 D9

More in the Solow Room

1977
Hartwick's Rule for Intergenerational Equity

If a society invests resource rents from exhaustible resource extraction into reproducible capital, per-capita consumption can be kept constant over time. A clean, usable sustainability condition derived directly from Solow's framework.

John M. Hartwick American Economic Review 1977 Q0 O4
1979
Economic Theory and Exhaustible Resources

The definitive theoretical treatment of exhaustible and renewable resource economics — optimal extraction paths, royalties, backstop technologies, and the welfare economics of resource depletion over time. Still the reference that graduate students are sent to first.

Partha Dasgupta & Geoffrey Heal Cambridge University Press 1979 Q3 C6
1992
Optimal Control Theory and Static Optimization in Economics

The mathematical backbone for dynamic resource models — optimal control, Pontryagin's maximum principle, and static optimisation, presented for economists. A reference that underpins a significant share of the theoretical apparatus across the Solow Room and the Long Equilibrium.

Daniel Léonard & Ngo Van Long Cambridge University Press 1992 C6 Q3
1994
On the Discount Rate for Public Investment

How should the government discount future costs and benefits when evaluating long-lived investments? Weitzman establishes the conditions under which the social discount rate diverges from the market rate — with direct implications for climate policy evaluation and infrastructure appraisal.

Martin L. Weitzman American Economic Review 1994 H4 Q5
